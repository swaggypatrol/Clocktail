The curve, by hand. Ask your agent for the curve editor and a window opens on your desktop: your card's V/F curve, light on dark, crisp at 200 %. Drag the points. The crashes sit on the points they happened at, the highest setting each point was ever marked stable at is a green mark, and where the card actually ran is a haze of dots. Click save and it's your daily profile.

**Why**

- **An agent can nudge a curve all night; the shape at the end wants a hand.** Fifteen MHz at a time through a chat is no way to draw a knee.
- **The record was in a table.** Twenty-eight crashes and a month of running points, each on the curve point it belongs to, say more drawn than listed.

**Now**

- **`curve_editor`** opens the window and returns at once. Opening it writes nothing. Call it again to bring the window to the front, or to read how it ended.
- **What you see:** the curve as the driver runs it (each point at the highest landed clock at or below it, so a dip never hides a cap), the stock line, the curve as it was when the window opened, every crash (solid when the curve was the trial, thin when XBAR, the memory or the wall was), each point's highest setting marked stable, and the last 60 days of samples under load, brighter where the card spent more time.
- **The low-voltage cliff becomes a transition band.** From the foot of the cliff (where the offsets start rising) to its top, the offsets follow a smoothstep in voltage. Nothing under the foot is lifted, nothing above the top is touched, so the lift is bounded by the cliff you already had. Both ends are lines you can drag: the foot left for a gentler slope (and more lift at low voltage), right up to the top for no band at all. The band is remembered beside your records and shown again as long as the card still holds that curve.
- **Above the band you draw.** A dragged point stays visible (the points under it, down to the band's top, land no higher). Shift drags everything above it along. One button smooths, and never lowers the flat top. The header keeps count: the steepest slope, how far above the opened curve you are, and how many points sit at or above a setting that crashed in a curve trial.
- **The click is the yes.** 确认并保存为 Daily writes the curve as one trial (opening a session itself when none is open), reads it back, keeps it as your daily profile with whatever other trial settings are in force (named in the window beforehand), and closes. If the card's curve or those settings changed while the window was open, it refuses and asks you to reopen. Every other way out of the window writes nothing.
- **The server is built for net10.0-windows** (the window is WinForms); the tests, and Linux, keep net10.0. The exe is 15 MB bigger for it.

**Known limits**

- The window lives in the server process. If your agent disconnects with it open, it goes; a save in flight gets 30 s to finish, and a trial it left on the card is reported on the next `curve_editor` call for `back_to_daily` to clear.
- The band is about looks, not stability: under the knee no card is tested, and Blackwell is most voltage-sensitive there. Keep it narrow unless you have reason not to.
- One window per server; two agents are two servers.

Upgrade: `npx -y clocktail@latest setup`, then restart your agents. Your card and profiles stay as they are.
