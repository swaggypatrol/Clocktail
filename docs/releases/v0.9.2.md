The memory tuner now stops on failure-record requests whose outcome is unknown, and considers a useful compromise between peak bandwidth and lowest latency.

- **A lost reply is unknown.** A crash-record request that times out, loses its pipe or returns no response no longer permits more candidate trials. This covers both the scan and the A B B A comparison path, including a record committed successfully whose reply was lost. The job ends with `best_mhz` null and `confirmed` false and still attempts normal restoration; verify the failure record and recovery before starting another job. A positively acknowledged failure can still be followed by comparisons of other candidates.
- **Keep the failure's identity.** `lab_status` adds `failure_recording`: its `state` is `confirmed`, `rejected` or `unknown`, `detail` explains an unresolved outcome, and `trial_id` identifies the measured trial. This makes an unresolved record visible without treating a timeout as proof it was either saved or rejected.
- **Measure the compromise too.** When the plateau/bandwidth/latency choices leave a slot free, the tuner includes a nondominated compromise between bandwidth and latency. It still measures at most three candidates and applies the same paired comparisons and final confirmation. In the review's simulated example, the useful +100 MHz candidate now gets measured instead of being omitted between the +50 and +150 MHz extremes. Those offsets describe a regression test, not recommended settings for another GPU.
