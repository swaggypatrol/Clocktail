Clock locks checked against what actually ran, and the other clock domains in view.

- **A clock lock is now checked, not trusted.** A cap, floor, V/F point lock or Boost Lock is a request to the driver, which still lowers the clock under it for power, temperature and voltage. `stress_test`, `watch` and the live watch now report `clock_locks`: each lock in force against the core clock actually sampled, with the share of samples that ran off it. A floor the driver derated under says the run did not test that clock throughout. Locks left by `nvidia-smi -lgc` or Afterburner are checked the same way.
- **The live watch notices a lock coming and going.** Set or released mid-session, by Clocktail or another tool, it gets its own line, and the summary compares a lock only with the samples taken while it was in force. The CSV gains `sys_mhz`, `video_mhz` and `clock_locks` columns.
- **XBAR, SYS and Video beside the core.** `gpu_status` reports the three domain clocks (`domain_clocks_mhz`) and any lock in force. `watch` and the live lines add SYS, and their totals add Video; `stress_test` adds Video. A held core clock says nothing about these domains, so a change to one can now be checked against the others.
- **No hard P-state lock, on purpose.** The manual now explains why Clocktail does not write NVIDIA's diagnostic hard lock: it removes the driver's own derating, and on the production driver it has been seen to starve the other clock domains and hang the GPU. `set_clock_lock` stays on the stable partner "Client" interface that `nvidia-smi` and Afterburner use.
- **README:** the V/F curve editor has its own section in English and Chinese.

Upgrade: `npx -y clocktail@latest setup`, then restart your agents. Nothing is written to your card by upgrading; your profiles and history stay as they are.
