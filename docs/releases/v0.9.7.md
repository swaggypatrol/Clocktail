The elevated Clocktail service now asks for itself before a daily profile changes. Keeping, adopting, loading, saving or deleting a profile, setting the daily power limit, and stock or factory each show a Windows confirmation from the service, listing the settings. No is the default; the request is refused after 60 seconds, and also when no one is at the desktop. The service checks the previewed values again before saving, so anything that changed while it waited is not kept. This comes after the agent's own confirmation, so expect two prompts. Going back to the daily profile you already have (`back_to_daily`, the cleanup after a job) does not ask. A keep sent straight to the service's pipe must now carry the preview's fingerprint.

The service no longer takes the lab's path from an environment variable; it only starts the `clocktail-lab.exe` installed next to it. Writes are matched by the registered kind and key, so a different capitalization can no longer get a power limit around its dedicated path or a trial-only setting into a daily profile. Setting the daily power limit keeps the record of settings adopted from another tool.

The service treats a damaged or unrecognized ownership record, or a damaged backup of one, as unreadable instead of as nothing owed, and will not overwrite it without an explicit recovery. A damaged or hand-edited `profile.json` is reported as damaged, and one bad saved preset no longer hides the others. The pipe answers malformed requests with an error and times out clients that stop reading. Its logs are read from the end, and `broker.log` rotates at 10 MiB, keeping three copies. When the service stops, it waits for its background work to finish before releasing the driver.

Uninstall now asks you to confirm in the service's dialog when it puts the settings back. If you say no, the card returns to its original settings at the next restart.

Windows and Linux CI ran 1,597 .NET tests plus the installer tests, with the GPU, the driver and the dialog simulated. On an RTX 5090 with the installed service, the dialog appeared. Yes saved a test profile and No left everything unchanged. At startup the service took over the previous service's settings and put the daily profile back on, as before. The 60-second timeout and the locked-screen refusal were tested only in simulation.

Upgrade with `npx -y clocktail@latest setup` and restart the MCP client.
