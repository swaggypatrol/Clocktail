# MSVDD and XBAR
The interconnect rail and the crossbar clock: the free +2 %, the +6-7 % with XBAR, and the cliff to stay away from.

Since 0.3.5 no tool sets MSVDD's (or NVVDD's) offsets: driver 621.01 takes them and changes nothing. MSVDD's ceiling moves only with the voltage boost (below). The REL measurements were made on 617.42; they show how XBAR is held, which is why the boost pays.

## MSVDD REL: NVIDIA's own reliability limit
- Stock MSVDD REL is -50 mV (MAX 1045 mV). Raising it to 0 (MAX 1095 mV) lets XBAR run faster where MSVDD was holding it: XBAR at 50 % load 2340 -> 2385 -> 2430 MHz and at full load 2190 -> 2240 -> 2285 MHz for REL -50 -> -25 -> 0 (about +45 MHz per 25 mV).
- Result on the reference card: **Blender Cycles 1.9-2.3 % faster at REL 0**, memory bandwidth unchanged, MSVDD about 2 W more, so the core runs 15-23 MHz lower under the 600 W wall; net still about +2 %. It passed every bit-exact test and nvgwls at 50 % and 100 % load.
- REL +20 adds about 1 % more and is past NVIDIA's limit (MAX 1115 mV via ALT/OP). Past +20 ALT went up with REL (MAX is the lowest of REL, ALT and OV), to REL +50 / ALT +30 = 1145 mV. With the ratio at 0.95 that headroom is what let XBAR's offset pay (below).
- The opposite, an MSVDD undervolt (617.42): REL -175 saved ~10 W (MSVDD 935 -> 830 mV) that the wall gave to the core (+40-60 MHz), but XBAR fell ~300 MHz (2182 -> ~1880). Games that lean on L2/crossbar bandwidth may lose more than they gain; untested. MSVDD tracks the core's demand minus REL, down to its VMIN floor (825 mV stock).

## The voltage boost: the ceiling that still moves
- `set_voltage_boost` raises every rail's REL limit 5 mV per 25 %: on the reference card on driver 621.01, NVVDD 1095 / 1100 / 1105 / 1110 / 1115 mV and MSVDD 1045 / 1050 / 1055 / 1060 / 1065 mV at 0 / 25 / 50 / 75 / 100 % (2026-10-23). 100 % adds the 20 mV between REL and ALT/OP; 1 % and 10 % are under the first step, which is why they moved nothing. It still writes on 621.01: it is the byte beside the offsets the driver drops, not one of them.
- At the daily XBAR +400 / +50 mV and ratio 0.9, 100 % let MSVDD run 1045-1050 mV under load where 1030 had been its top, and XBAR 2692-2700 MHz where it had been 2662. The L2 test read 4795, 4838 and 4843 GB/s at 100 % against 4779, 4779 and 4777 at 0 %, B A B A with nothing else on the GPU: 1.0-1.3 % faster in every pair, the core unchanged, DRAM within its spread.
- NVVDD's limit goes up with it, which a curve cut flat under it never reaches; a curve whose top sits above MAX gets those points back (the top at the rail's ALT/OP, NVIDIA's own operating limit).

## XBAR clock offset and voltage demand
- The XBAR offset raises XBAR at the same voltage (+75 MHz moved XBAR 2340 -> 2422 MHz with nothing else changing).
- Under load XBAR is held to **about 0.9 x the core clock**: the driver's clock ratio (0.8999 stock). About +250 MHz of offset reaches that; more only lowers the MSVDD voltage XBAR needs.
- **The XBAR offset pulls the SYS clock down** in the memory phase: XBAR 0 / +200 / +300 / +400 gave SYS 2182 / 2017 / 1912 / 1830 MHz. Giving SYS +150 back (`adjust_clock_domain sys`) at XBAR +300 changed nothing in a Blender render (A B B A, 5.846 / 5.846 / 5.832 / 5.834 s, 2026-10-21); its effect on games is not measured. Read `sys_mhz` in stress_test and watch when judging an XBAR step.
- The **voltage demand** (-25..+50 mV) raises MSVDD for XBAR's points. It is what keeps MSVDD off its floor at large offsets.

Blender Cycles (2560x1440, 384 spp, median of 7, lower is better), stock 6.774 s:
| Setting | Time | vs stock |
|---|---|---|
| REL 0 | 6.62-6.65 s | ~ -2 % |
| REL 0, XBAR +250, demand 0 | 6.345 s | -6.3 % |
| REL 0, XBAR +300, demand +20 mV | 6.33-6.39 s | -6 to -7 % |
| REL 0, XBAR +400, demand +30 mV | 6.322 s | -6.7 % |

## The ratio: XBAR's ceiling
- `set_clock_ratio` moves the 0.9: the driver's clock propagation ratio from the core (NVVDD) to XBAR (MSVDD). A higher ratio lets XBAR follow the core closer, about +29 MHz of XBAR per +0.01 at a 2900 MHz core, and asks MSVDD for the voltage that clock needs.
- On the reference card at the 600 W wall: 0.92 ran XBAR 2595 MHz against a 2850 MHz core (0.911); stock ran 2535 / 2865 (0.885). MSVDD rose 980 → 1005 mV and the core gave 15 MHz back to the wall. Its gain in frames or Blender is not measured.
- When: after MSVDD REL 0 and XBAR +250..+300 with its demand, if XBAR under load still sits at 0.9 x the core. 0.025 per call. The demand rules below apply to the clock XBAR then reaches; judge each step A B B A with a benchmark that uses memory.

## The cliff
- With a large XBAR offset and no extra demand, memory-bound work lets MSVDD fall to its floor (825 mV). When it sits there, the **core is pinned near 1610 MHz and bandwidth drops 14-16 %** (1460 -> 1221-1259 GB/s). At demand 0: clean from +0 to +250, marginal at +300 (an occasional drop to 1687 MHz), the cliff at +350 and +400. No hysteresis: back down, back to normal.
- Raising VMIN does not fix it (MSVDD then sits on the new floor, core still ~1620 MHz). **The voltage demand does**: at +400, demand +20 mV kept MSVDD at 845 mV and restored 1446 GB/s and a 2445 MHz core.
- +20 mV holds only up to +300. At +400 the memory phase's P10 core still fell to 1942 MHz with +20 mV and 1830 MHz with +30; only +50 mV kept it clean (2392). So past +300 the demand must be +50 mV, and +400 was no faster than +300 in Blender anyway.
- The firmware mechanism is unknown. Stay at +250-300 with +20 mV, which is what the founders preset holds (XBAR +300, +20 mV).
- An overclocker reported +450 MHz without extra voltage giving a grey screen and artefacts.
- **If anything is unstable with XBAR raised, step XBAR down 50 MHz at a time** before touching the curve.

## The tuner (`tune_uncore`)
- It searches what this topic describes, on this card: XBAR's offset a step at a time with the demand the cliff rule asks for (+20 mV above +250, +50 above +300; more when a step still hits the cliff), then the ratio, then the voltage boost (MSVDD REL until 0.3.5), then the ratio and the boost a step each together (the window below), each candidate a trial compared A B B A against the best so far, in rounds: an axis runs again when another has moved the best since it last ran.
- The measure is the lab's L2 test: a 32 MiB region (a third of the 5090's L2) filled and verified over and over, so the traffic is the crossbar's and not the DRAM's (~4500 GB/s at the daily profile on the reference card, against ~1575 for DRAM). A candidate is kept when it is faster in both pairs and by 0.5 % or more, DRAM bandwidth does not fall, and the memory phase's core clock does not fall 1 % (one V/F bin is ~0.5 %; 5 % is the cliff, which gets more demand). Runs of one setting more than 2 % apart were disturbed (other work, or a card still settling): the comparison is measured again, whole, and left unjudged (which ends the search) when it is disturbed twice. The best is named only when its L2 gain holds once more against the daily settings, in a comparison of its own at the end (stage `confirm`); a core or DRAM cost there past what one step may have is told with it, for a game or benchmark A B B A to decide. A search that ends early (lab_stop, disturbed twice) names nothing.
- What it found on the reference card (daily 600 W, XBAR +250, MSVDD REL 0, the core ~3050 MHz in P1): under load XBAR ran in a window ~30 MHz wide, its floor the ratio x the core (0.9 x 3045 = 2740) and its ceiling the clock MSVDD's reliability limit allows (2760-2790 MHz: `card_settings` perf_limits, `RELIABILITY_MSVDD_0`). So XBAR's offset alone bought nothing (+300 and +350), and the ratio alone hurt: at 0.925 XBAR could not follow it and the core fell 1.9 %. MSVDD REL +10 mV lifted the ceiling (+0.5 % L2), then ratio 0.925 (+0.5 %), then XBAR +300 / +20 mV (+1.7 %): about +2.8 % L2 with the core where it was. +350 / +50 mV was slower again (-1.7 %). That was a short check (15 s runs).
- The full run (defaults, 20 s runs, 10 comparisons, 18 minutes; the GPU otherwise idle) found the same coupling and a different best. Alone, nothing paid: XBAR +300 and +350, the ratio 0.925 and 0.95 (at 0.95 the core fell 3.5 %), MSVDD REL +10 and +20, each within ±0.6 %. Together, ratio 0.925 with REL +10 gave +1.25 % L2 in both pairs, then 0.95 with +20 another +1.21 %, DRAM +0.5 %. XBAR +300 / +20 mV on top gave +2.0 % L2 more but cost the core 1.3 %, so it was not kept. Best: XBAR +250 / 0 mV, ratio 0.95, MSVDD REL +20 mV, about +2.5 % L2. The memory phase's core read -0.9 and -0.5 % on the two kept steps, inside its spread (2880-3052 MHz run to run), so a game or benchmark A B B A is what says whether it pays. Games not measured.
- Past REL +20 (the person allowed +50; MSVDD's top is the lowest of REL and ALT, 1095 and 1115 mV with no offsets, so ALT went up with REL, 20 mV behind): 14 settings each against ratio 0.95 / REL +20, A B B A, 20 s runs, the GPU idle (0 % after every run). REL +30, +40, +50: +3.1, +3.5, +3.9 % L2. With REL +50 XBAR's offset paid at last: +300 / +20 mV +4.7 %, +400 / +50 mV +5.6 %, +500 / +50 mV +5.8 %. DRAM +0.1 to +0.3 %, the core inside its spread. Below that headroom XBAR did not pay: lower cost L2 (+200 -1.3 %, +150 -2.8 %), demand alone too (-1.0 %), and the window's two halves apart bought nothing. The daily settings read -0.9 % against ratio 0.95 / REL +20, so the best is about +6.7 % L2 over daily: XBAR +500 / +50 mV, ratio 0.95, MSVDD REL +50 / ALT +30 mV (+400 / +50 mV is within 0.2 % of it). XBAR is held by MSVDD's voltage limit; given the headroom, its offset pays. Every run passed the lab's bit checks; `stress_test` and games not run.
- It keeps nothing: `lab_status` names the best setting; set it with `adjust_xbar`, `set_clock_ratio` and `set_voltage_boost`, then `lab_test`, `stress_test` and games, then `keep_as_daily` (ask). Its gains in games are not measured; the reference card's XBAR +300 with +20 mV made Blender 6-7 % faster.

## Driver 621.01: MSVDD at stock
- 621.01 (2026-10-22) takes every rail write and changes nothing: NVVDD and MSVDD, up and down, after a reboot too (the call answers OK, the readback is the same). A report traces it to a flag the driver sets when it loads, which its rail setter honours by skipping the offsets. `card_settings` lists the rails' offsets as not offered, and since 0.3.5 no tool sets them; the daily profile goes on without its uncore group when it holds MSVDD offsets (`manual troubleshooting`). XBAR's offset and demand, the ratio, the curve, memory, power, current limits (lowered, read back) and fans still write.
- So MSVDD stays at REL -50 (MAX 1045 mV, XBAR's ceiling ~2445 MHz under `RELIABILITY_MSVDD_0`), and `tune_uncore` moves the voltage boost instead (until 0.3.5 it held MSVDD by a limit at the card's offset). Each setting against XBAR +100 / 0 mV at ratio 0.9, A B B A, 20 s runs, nothing else on the GPU (the four runs of a setting within 0.5 %): stock 0 -3.5 % L2 (DRAM -2.5 %), +150 +2.3 %, +200 +3.5 %, +250 / 0 mV +5.1 % (DRAM +2.9 %), +300 / +20 mV +5.5 %, +350 / +50 mV +5.0 %, **+400 / +50 mV +6.5 %** (DRAM +3.2 %, the core -0.1 %). The ratio did not pay: at +100, 0.925 and 0.95 slowed the memory phase's core 2.0 and 2.4 % with L2 flat. Kept on the reference card: XBAR +400 / +50 mV at ratio 0.9, about 10 % L2 over stock; `lab_test` 120 s passed bit for bit, burn 2917 MHz at 599 W (2925 at +100), L2 4680 GB/s, DRAM 1698 GB/s (1799 with the 617.42 uncore at the same memory offset: the crossbar holds the memory). The 617.42 settings are the saved profile '617.42 full uncore' there.
- With the boost in the search (0.3.5, 2026-10-23), from the daily XBAR +400 / +50 mV, ratio 0.9, boost 0, A B B A, 20 s runs, a setting's runs within 0.1 % of each other: XBAR +450 gave +1.5 % L2, +500 at ratio 0.9 nothing; the ratio alone (0.925, 0.95) and the boost alone (25, 50 %) nothing or a cost; the two together (0.95 and 50 %) +0.8 %, and then XBAR +500 paid +4.0 % and boost 100 % +0.7 %. Measured again against the daily: **XBAR +500 / +50 mV, ratio 0.95, boost 100 %: L2 +7.1 %, DRAM +4.5 %, the memory phase's core -0.9 %.** `lab_test` 180 s passed bit for bit (L2 4984 GB/s, DRAM 1767, XBAR 2812 MHz; burn 2902 MHz at 599 W against 2917), and `stress_test` passed at load 100 (2820 MHz at 600 W, MSVDD 935 mV and 64 W, XBAR 2535 MHz) and at 50 (2970 MHz, XBAR 2760). The boost opens XBAR's window as MSVDD's REL did on 617.42.
- From there, XBAR +320 to +590 in 30 MHz steps (+50 mV, ratio 0.95, boost 100 %), each A B B A against +500 (2026-10-23): L2 peaked at **+560** (+0.98 %, and +1.05 % measured again), +590 less (+0.85 %). +560 / +50 mV passed `lab_test` 180 s bit for bit (L2 5053 GB/s) and `stress_test` 120 s at load 100 (2827 MHz, XBAR 2572 at 600 W) and 50 (2970 MHz, XBAR 2805); it is the reference card's daily since. Games not measured. `card_report` counts XBAR up to +560 as measured, with the demand the cliff asks for.
- A run disturbed in the middle reads low without the lab seeing it: the first search on 621.01 ran while the agent restarted and builds ran, one run in each comparison read 15-18 % low, and it kept +100 and turned down +150 and +200 on noise. The lab checks for other work before and just after a run, not during it. Since 0.3.4 the tuner reads the four runs itself: runs of one setting more than 2 % apart are measured again, and the best is measured against the daily settings once more before it is named. A smaller reading-off (a run 1-2 % low) can still carry one comparison on the way, as in 0.3.3; that last comparison is what keeps it from being named. Still, while a tuner runs, leave the machine alone: a disturbed comparison measured twice ends the search.

## Reading it
`card_settings` shows each domain's offset and demand and MSVDD's REL/ALT/OV/VMIN offsets and limits; `gpu_status` shows MSVDD's mV, amps and watts; `stress_test` and `watch` report the XBAR clock under load. `card_settings` `clock_ratio` shows the ratio. `set_voltage_boost`, `adjust_xbar`, `set_clock_ratio` and `adjust_clock_domain` change them (`manual method`, phase 6). A memory-bound game on a large XBAR offset with MSVDD at exactly 825 mV and the core stuck near 1600 MHz is the cliff.
