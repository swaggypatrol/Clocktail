# MSVDD and XBAR
The interconnect rail and the crossbar clock: the free +2 %, the +6-7 % with XBAR, and the cliff to stay away from.

## MSVDD REL: NVIDIA's own reliability limit
- Stock MSVDD REL is -50 mV (MAX 1045 mV). Raising it to 0 (MAX 1095 mV) lets XBAR run faster where MSVDD was holding it: XBAR at 50 % load 2340 -> 2385 -> 2430 MHz and at full load 2190 -> 2240 -> 2285 MHz for REL -50 -> -25 -> 0 (about +45 MHz per 25 mV).
- Result on the reference card: **Blender Cycles 1.9-2.3 % faster at REL 0**, memory bandwidth unchanged, MSVDD about 2 W more, so the core runs 15-23 MHz lower under the 600 W wall; net still about +2 %. It passed every bit-exact test and nvgwls at 50 % and 100 % load.
- REL +20 adds about 1 % more and is past NVIDIA's limit (MAX 1115 mV via ALT/OP). Past +20, `adjust_msvdd` takes ALT up with REL (MAX is the lowest of REL, ALT and OV), to REL +50 / ALT +30 = 1145 mV at most (`CLOCKTAIL_MCP_MSVDD_REL_MAX_MV`, default 50). With the ratio at 0.95 that headroom is what let XBAR's offset pay (below).
- The opposite, an MSVDD undervolt (`adjust_msvdd`): REL -175 saved ~10 W (MSVDD 935 -> 830 mV) that the wall gave to the core (+40-60 MHz), but XBAR fell ~300 MHz (2182 -> ~1880). Games that lean on L2/crossbar bandwidth may lose more than they gain; untested. MSVDD tracks the core's demand minus REL, down to its VMIN floor (825 mV stock).

## XBAR clock offset and voltage demand
- The XBAR offset raises XBAR at the same voltage (+75 MHz moved XBAR 2340 -> 2422 MHz with nothing else changing).
- Under load XBAR is held to **about 0.9 x the core clock**: the driver's clock ratio (0.8999 stock). About +250 MHz of offset reaches that; more only lowers the MSVDD voltage XBAR needs.
- **The XBAR offset pulls the SYS clock down** in the memory phase: XBAR 0 / +200 / +300 / +400 gave SYS 2182 / 2017 / 1912 / 1830 MHz. Giving SYS +150 back (`adjust_clock_domain sys`) at XBAR +300 changed nothing in a Blender render (A B B A, 5.846 / 5.846 / 5.832 / 5.834 s, 2026-10-21); its effect on games is not measured. Read `sys_mhz` in stress_test and watch when judging an XBAR step.
- The **voltage demand** (-25..+50 mV) raises MSVDD for XBAR's points. It is what keeps MSVDD off its floor at large offsets.

Blender Cycles (2560x1440, 384 spp, median of 7, lower is better), stock 6.774 s:
| Setting | Time | vs stock |
|---|---|---|
| REL 0 | 6.62-6.65 s | ~ -2 % |
| REL 0, XBAR +250, demand 0 | 6.345 s | -6.3 % |
| REL 0, XBAR +300, demand +20 mV | 6.33-6.39 s | -6 to -7 % |
| REL 0, XBAR +400, demand +30 mV | 6.322 s | -6.7 % |

## The ratio: XBAR's ceiling
- `set_clock_ratio` moves the 0.9: the driver's clock propagation ratio from the core (NVVDD) to XBAR (MSVDD). A higher ratio lets XBAR follow the core closer, about +29 MHz of XBAR per +0.01 at a 2900 MHz core, and asks MSVDD for the voltage that clock needs.
- On the reference card at the 600 W wall: 0.92 ran XBAR 2595 MHz against a 2850 MHz core (0.911); stock ran 2535 / 2865 (0.885). MSVDD rose 980 → 1005 mV and the core gave 15 MHz back to the wall. Its gain in frames or Blender is not measured.
- When: after MSVDD REL 0 and XBAR +250..+300 with its demand, if XBAR under load still sits at 0.9 x the core. 0.025 per call. The demand rules below apply to the clock XBAR then reaches; judge each step A B B A with a benchmark that uses memory.

## The cliff
- With a large XBAR offset and no extra demand, memory-bound work lets MSVDD fall to its floor (825 mV). When it sits there, the **core is pinned near 1610 MHz and bandwidth drops 14-16 %** (1460 -> 1221-1259 GB/s). At demand 0: clean from +0 to +250, marginal at +300 (an occasional drop to 1687 MHz), the cliff at +350 and +400. No hysteresis: back down, back to normal.
- Raising VMIN does not fix it (MSVDD then sits on the new floor, core still ~1620 MHz). **The voltage demand does**: at +400, demand +20 mV kept MSVDD at 845 mV and restored 1446 GB/s and a 2445 MHz core.
- +20 mV holds only up to +300. At +400 the memory phase's P10 core still fell to 1942 MHz with +20 mV and 1830 MHz with +30; only +50 mV kept it clean (2392). So past +300 the demand must be +50 mV, and +400 was no faster than +300 in Blender anyway.
- The firmware mechanism is unknown. Stay at +250-300 with +20 mV, which is what the founders preset holds (XBAR +300, +20 mV).
- An overclocker reported +450 MHz without extra voltage giving a grey screen and artefacts.
- **If anything is unstable with XBAR raised, step XBAR down 50 MHz at a time** before touching the curve.

## The tuner (`tune_uncore`)
- It searches what this topic describes, on this card: XBAR's offset a step at a time with the demand the cliff rule asks for (+20 mV above +250, +50 above +300; more when a step still hits the cliff), then the ratio, then MSVDD REL, then the ratio and MSVDD a step each together (the window below), each candidate a trial compared A B B A against the best so far, in rounds: an axis runs again when another has moved the best since it last ran.
- The measure is the lab's L2 test: a 32 MiB region (a third of the 5090's L2) filled and verified over and over, so the traffic is the crossbar's and not the DRAM's (~4500 GB/s at the daily profile on the reference card, against ~1575 for DRAM). A candidate is kept when it is faster in both pairs and by 0.5 % or more, DRAM bandwidth does not fall, and the memory phase's core clock does not fall 1 % (one V/F bin is ~0.5 %; 5 % is the cliff, which gets more demand).
- What it found on the reference card (daily 600 W, XBAR +250, MSVDD REL 0, the core ~3050 MHz in P1): under load XBAR ran in a window ~30 MHz wide, its floor the ratio x the core (0.9 x 3045 = 2740) and its ceiling the clock MSVDD's reliability limit allows (2760-2790 MHz: `card_settings` perf_limits, `RELIABILITY_MSVDD_0`). So XBAR's offset alone bought nothing (+300 and +350), and the ratio alone hurt: at 0.925 XBAR could not follow it and the core fell 1.9 %. MSVDD REL +10 mV lifted the ceiling (+0.5 % L2), then ratio 0.925 (+0.5 %), then XBAR +300 / +20 mV (+1.7 %): about +2.8 % L2 with the core where it was. +350 / +50 mV was slower again (-1.7 %). That was a short check (15 s runs).
- The full run (defaults, 20 s runs, 10 comparisons, 18 minutes; the GPU otherwise idle) found the same coupling and a different best. Alone, nothing paid: XBAR +300 and +350, the ratio 0.925 and 0.95 (at 0.95 the core fell 3.5 %), MSVDD REL +10 and +20, each within ±0.6 %. Together, ratio 0.925 with REL +10 gave +1.25 % L2 in both pairs, then 0.95 with +20 another +1.21 %, DRAM +0.5 %. XBAR +300 / +20 mV on top gave +2.0 % L2 more but cost the core 1.3 %, so it was not kept. Best: XBAR +250 / 0 mV, ratio 0.95, MSVDD REL +20 mV, about +2.5 % L2. The memory phase's core read -0.9 and -0.5 % on the two kept steps, inside its spread (2880-3052 MHz run to run), so a game or benchmark A B B A is what says whether it pays. Games not measured.
- Past REL +20 (the person allowed +50; MSVDD's top is the lowest of REL and ALT, 1095 and 1115 mV with no offsets, so ALT went up with REL, 20 mV behind): 14 settings each against ratio 0.95 / REL +20, A B B A, 20 s runs, the GPU idle (0 % after every run). REL +30, +40, +50: +3.1, +3.5, +3.9 % L2. With REL +50 XBAR's offset paid at last: +300 / +20 mV +4.7 %, +400 / +50 mV +5.6 %, +500 / +50 mV +5.8 %. DRAM +0.1 to +0.3 %, the core inside its spread. Below that headroom XBAR did not pay: lower cost L2 (+200 -1.3 %, +150 -2.8 %), demand alone too (-1.0 %), and the window's two halves apart bought nothing. The daily settings read -0.9 % against ratio 0.95 / REL +20, so the best is about +6.7 % L2 over daily: XBAR +500 / +50 mV, ratio 0.95, MSVDD REL +50 / ALT +30 mV (+400 / +50 mV is within 0.2 % of it). XBAR is held by MSVDD's voltage limit; given the headroom, its offset pays. Every run passed the lab's bit checks; `stress_test` and games not run.
- It keeps nothing: `lab_status` names the best setting; set it with `adjust_xbar`, `set_clock_ratio` and `adjust_msvdd`, then `lab_test`, `stress_test` and games, then `keep_as_daily` (ask). Its gains in games are not measured; the reference card's XBAR +300 with +20 mV made Blender 6-7 % faster.

## Driver 621.01: MSVDD at stock
- 621.01 (2026-10-22) takes every rail write and changes nothing: NVVDD and MSVDD, up and down, after a reboot too (the call answers OK, the readback is the same). A report traces it to a flag the driver sets when it loads, which its rail setter honours by skipping the offsets. `card_settings` marks both rail controls as not settable there and `adjust_msvdd` / `adjust_nvvdd` refuse; the daily profile goes on without its uncore group when it holds MSVDD offsets (`manual troubleshooting`). XBAR's offset and demand, the ratio, the curve, memory, power, current limits (lowered, read back) and fans still write.
- So MSVDD stays at REL -50 (MAX 1045 mV, XBAR's ceiling ~2445 MHz under `RELIABILITY_MSVDD_0`), and `tune_uncore` holds it there (its MSVDD limit defaults to the card's offset on that driver). Each setting against XBAR +100 / 0 mV at ratio 0.9, A B B A, 20 s runs, nothing else on the GPU (the four runs of a setting within 0.5 %): stock 0 -3.5 % L2 (DRAM -2.5 %), +150 +2.3 %, +200 +3.5 %, +250 / 0 mV +5.1 % (DRAM +2.9 %), +300 / +20 mV +5.5 %, +350 / +50 mV +5.0 %, **+400 / +50 mV +6.5 %** (DRAM +3.2 %, the core -0.1 %). The ratio did not pay: at +100, 0.925 and 0.95 slowed the memory phase's core 2.0 and 2.4 % with L2 flat. Kept on the reference card: XBAR +400 / +50 mV at ratio 0.9, about 10 % L2 over stock; `lab_test` 120 s passed bit for bit, burn 2917 MHz at 599 W (2925 at +100), L2 4680 GB/s, DRAM 1698 GB/s (1799 with the 617.42 uncore at the same memory offset: the crossbar holds the memory). The 617.42 settings are the saved profile '617.42 full uncore' there.
- A run disturbed in the middle reads low without the lab seeing it: the first search on 621.01 ran while the agent restarted and builds ran, one run in each comparison read 15-18 % low, and it kept +100 and turned down +150 and +200 on noise. The lab checks for other work before and just after a run, not during it. While a tuner runs, leave the machine alone, and read the four runs of a comparison (`lab_status`): runs of the same setting more than ~2 % apart were disturbed.

## Reading it
`card_settings` shows each domain's offset and demand and MSVDD's REL/ALT/OV/VMIN offsets and limits; `gpu_status` shows MSVDD's mV, amps and watts; `stress_test` and `watch` report the XBAR clock under load. `card_settings` `clock_ratio` shows the ratio. `adjust_msvdd`, `adjust_xbar`, `set_clock_ratio` and `adjust_clock_domain` change them (`manual method`, phase 6). A memory-bound game on a large XBAR offset with MSVDD at exactly 825 mV and the core stuck near 1600 MHz is the cliff.
